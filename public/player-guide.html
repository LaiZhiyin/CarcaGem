01 / BEFORE YOU START

Choose your crew, then set sail.

The goal is simple: place tiles, claim features, and finish with the highest score.

A.

Local Game

Play against AI or take turns with friends on one device.

  1. Choose Local Game on the home screen, then select 2 or 3 players.
  2. Enter nicknames and give each player a different color.
  3. Choose Human or AI for each seat. Players take turns in order.
B.

Online Game

Play with friends from separate devices in a shared room.

  1. Choose Online Game, enter a nickname, and create a room.
  2. Share the invite code so friends can join your room.
  3. Choose a color and get ready. The host starts the game; take your turn when prompted.
Nemo color preview

Try another color. The animal stays the same.

Ocean-animal avatars are assigned at random. Each player in a room gets a different animal.Avatars identify players; colored dots on the board mark claimable positions. The avatar and player card stay in sync when you change colors.

03 / TAKE A TURN

Place a tile, send an explorer, then score.

You may place one explorer per turn, or keep it in reserve. The first player starts.

1.

Draw and Place a Tile

Select a translucent position, then click the tile or press R to rotate it.Every touching edge must match: sea to sea, beach to beach, and road to road.

Orientation: 0°
2.

Place an Explorer or Skip

Select an available dot on the new tile to claim that feature, or click the green ✓ or press Space, keep your explorer in reserve, and finish the turn.

✓
SeaRoadBeachLighthouse
3.

Complete a Feature and Score

When a road, beach, or lighthouse is completed, it scores automatically and its explorers return to their players. The next turn begins; explorers claimed at sea stay there until the end of the game.

→
Explorers return when a feature is completed

Tip: claim features that are likely to finish so your explorers can score more than once.

Check whether a feature is already claimed.You cannot add an explorer to a connected road, beach, or sea already claimed by any player, including you. Claim separate features first, then connect them with tiles. Connected features compare each player’s explorer count.
04 / FEATURES & SCORING

Four features, four ways to score.

In the top-down 3D tiles, boardwalks are roads and yellow land is usually beach. Claimable regions are marked with dots. Select a tile below to zoom in.

ROAD / BOARDWALK

Road

A road is complete when every open end connects to another road. Each distinct tile in the road scores distinct tiles point each.

When completed1 point per tile
Unfinished at game end1 point per tile

BEACH / YELLOW LAND

Beach

A beach is complete when all of its outward edges are connected with no open gaps.Each tile scores 2 points when completed. A beach does not need to form a circle.

When completed2 points per tile
Unfinished at game end1 point per tile

LIGHTHOUSE / BUILDING

Lighthouse

A lighthouse is complete when all 8 surrounding spaces, including the diagonals, contain any tile. That is 9 tiles including the lighthouse tile.

When all 8 surrounding spaces are filled9 points
Unfinished at game endSurrounding tiles + 1

SEA / BLUE WATER

Sea

Sea connects along tile edges and keeps expanding. At game end, score the number of different completed beaches. Explorers placed at sea cannot return before the game ends.

During the gameNo immediate score
At game endCompleted beaches × 3

Who scores a connected feature?

Count each player’s explorers in that feature.

The player with the most explorers scores the full value. Every player tied for the most also scores the full value; points are not split.For example, if a beach is worth 8 points and two players each have one explorer there, both score 8 points.

05 / SEA SCORING

Sea scores beaches, not spaces.

Find the connected sea you claimed, then count the completed beaches it touches.

Even if the sea touches the same completed beach in several places, it scores only 3 points for that beach.

Open beaches and lighthouses do not count

Count only completed beaches adjacent to the connected sea. Nearby or diagonal spaces do not count as connected.

Compare explorer counts at game end

Sea does not score immediately. The player with the most explorers gets the full score; tied players each get the full score.

06 / TREASURE & ENDGAME

Spot a gem and earn an extra turn.

Red gems mark treasure tiles. The reward depends on who occupies the feature when it is completed.

Complete a treasure feature to earn an extra turn.

  1. Look for a tile marked with a red gem.
  2. A completed road or beachthat contains a treasure tile and has at least one explorer earns a bonus turn.
  3. The player with the most explorers in that feature earns an extra turn. Every player tied for the most earns one too.

Drawing or placing a treasure tile does not trigger the reward by itself. Follow the turn prompt to continue.

When does the game end?The game ends after the last tile is placed and scored, or when no remaining tile can be placed legally. Then unfinished roads, beaches, and lighthouses score, along with all claimed seas.
RoadUnfinished: 1 point per tile
BeachUnfinished: 1 point per tile
LighthouseSurrounding tiles + 1
SeaDifferent completed beaches × 3

Features scored earlier are not scored again. Final points are added to the total score to determine the winner.

07 / STRATEGY

Give every explorer a purpose.

Learn the rules, then weigh rewards, completion odds, and remaining tiles.

01.

Keep an explorer in reserve

Try to keep 1–2 explorers available instead of committing all of them to hard-to-finish features. Short roads and easy-to-complete beaches return explorers sooner. Before placing one, check Remaining Tiles to see how many useful tiles remain and decide whether to keep investing.

02.

Sea is worth claiming, and worth waiting on

Check how many completed or nearly completed beaches this sea may touch. Explorers at sea are committed until the end, so do not claim it just because it is large.

03.

Count before connecting features

Connecting separate features can create competition. Before joining an opponent’s feature, count each player’s explorers and check for a treasure bonus.

08 / QUICK REFERENCE

Questions? Start here.

A few simple habits can make your first game much smoother.

Can I keep playing after I run out of explorers?

Yes. You can still place tiles, but you cannot place another explorer. Select the green check or Skip to continue. Explorers return when a road, beach, or lighthouse is completed.

Why can’t I select a dot, or why are there no dots?

Check that it is your turn, the tile has a legal position, and you have an explorer available. A connected feature already claimed by any player cannot be claimed again. Road intersections are not claimable features.

Can I draw another tile if there is no legal position?

The game checks legal positions for the current tile and draws another if none are available. If no remaining tile can be placed, the game ends. Select a translucent position and try matching orientations.

How do I see remaining tiles? Why does the game start at 78/80?

In local and online games, select the blue layered-tile icon beside Activity to open the 5×5 panel. The 80 tiles include the starting tile. At the start, one tile is on the board and one has been drawn, leaving 78 in the deck. Tile counts add up to the total shown. The 43/80 guide example is illustrative; the current game is the source of truth.

Can I undo a move?

Local games have an Undo button when a previous move is available. Remaining tile counts are restored after undo. Online games do not support undo. Returning home or refreshing may end your game; read the confirmation prompt.

Why does the avatar look different from the board markers?

The ocean-animal avatar identifies each player in setup and player cards. Explorers on the board use colored round markers. The color of a claimable dot indicates its feature type.

Where can I see score details?

Player cards on the right show current scores. Select Activity to review score updates. Sea points are added only at the end of the game.
